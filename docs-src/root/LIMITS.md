# Limits

GENERATED from `docs-src/root/LIMITS.md`. What this repository does **not** establish, stated plainly. See [`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md) for what it does establish, and the status of each claim.

1. **The rule's selection rests on one criterion, not on a deeper principle.** Two closed forms for the law pass every test in [`docs/tests.md`](docs/tests.md), including the no-signalling test, equally. The one test that separates them is whether `f(eta)` is exactly multiplicative under composition of independent records — rule (i) is, to {{ruleIMultiplicativeError}}; rule (ii) misses by up to {{ruleIIMultiplicativeMiss}}. Nothing in this repository derives multiplicativity from a more basic requirement; it is a criterion the proposal that motivated this work stated, not one forced by the law's own construction.

2. **The crystal-regime self-gravity frequency is imported, not derived.** `omega_SN`, and the formula relating it to a harmonic oscillator's frequency shift, come from the optomechanics literature (see `rcg.law.omega_sn_sq_from_shift`'s docstring and [`docs/related-work.md`](docs/related-work.md)). The adapter that reduces this repository's law to that formula assumes the self-gravity term enters the oscillator frequency squared linearly in the law's own cross-branch weight — plausible for a near-ground-state harmonic oscillator, but an assumption about that model, not a consequence of the law.

3. **The adapter's reduction to a state of rank above two is an extrapolation.** The law's closed forms are stated for two branches. A real oscillator's thermal state has none; the adapter reads "how much record exists" off the state's purity, which equals the two-branch overlap exactly for two branches and continues smoothly for anything more mixed, with the record read as "more than perfect" there. That continuation is forced by the chain of reasoning in `rcg.models.gaussian`'s module docstring, but it is additional to what the law itself states. [`docs/realistic.md`](docs/realistic.md)'s stage 4 checks this conclusion against an independent second reading (coherence at the lattice scale) and finds the two agree on both of the hot platforms the conclusion depends on — so the conclusion does not rest on this extrapolation — but the extrapolation itself remains unstated by the law.

4. **The two-body result is mean-field, not a full master equation.** The zero-entanglement prediction (T8, [`docs/tests.md`](docs/tests.md)) is checked under a mean-field version of the law acting on two qubits, not under a continuous master equation with decoherence and measurement backaction acting throughout. A full treatment is not attempted here.

5. **The environment is treated as a position record exclusively.** Every decoherence channel in [`rcg.models.gaussian`](../src/rcg/models/gaussian.py) is read as resolving the mass's position. A bath that instead recorded momentum would need a different filtering equation; this is not modelled.

6. **Classical force noise is not modelled.** Casimir forces and patch-potential noise, which any real platform in [`docs/realistic.md`](docs/realistic.md) would also face, add to the total diffusion rate and so would only strengthen this repository's negative feasibility result — but they are absent from the model, not included and found negligible.

7. **Two of the three platforms in `data/platforms.yaml` carry an assumed quality factor.** `torsion-mg`'s `Q=5` and `levitated-gs`'s `Q=1e8` are not published for those systems; both are swept over many orders of magnitude in `experiments/realistic/stage3_map.py`, and no conclusion in this repository depends on the exact value, but the single numbers quoted for those two platforms elsewhere do.

8. **The minimum-observation-time result is a special case of an already-published bound, not a new one.** [Helou2017] already derives a numerically fitted scaling law for the time needed to resolve a conditioned spectral feature in exactly this kind of experiment; this repository's `t_min = pi Q / omega_0` is a weaker, law-independent floor under that same family, not an independent discovery (see `docs/minimum-time.md`). It is also checked against only one published experiment: [Y25] is the only external data point available, and the agreement (predicted {{tMinPublishedTorsionDays}} days against a reported need for 300+) is suggestive, not a calibrated fit — a reader should treat it as one data point, not an established scaling law.

9. **Unconditional Schrödinger–Newton is not excluded by this repository, nor — as far as this repository's one citation shows — by any published experiment.** An earlier version of this work's write-up claimed otherwise, on no source; [`docs/realistic.md`](docs/realistic.md)'s stage 4 corrects that claim and states what the best published bound actually reports.
